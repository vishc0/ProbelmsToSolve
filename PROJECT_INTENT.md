# CloudSetup Project Intent

## Preamble

Artificial intelligence should not benefit only large technology companies,
specialized laboratories, or people who can afford expensive services. It should
help ordinary people handle the real difficulties of daily life with greater
safety, dignity, independence, and opportunity.

People already lose time, money, health, mobility, and confidence to scams,
inaccessible information, confusing paperwork, transportation barriers,
preventable costs, education gaps, small-business administration, and services
that are difficult to navigate. Many of these problems do not require
speculative future technology. They require careful application of capabilities
that already exist or are becoming inexpensive.

CloudSetup exists to find those problems, determine whether AI can responsibly
help, and turn the best responses into affordable, reusable projects that
communities, entrepreneurs, public institutions, and responsible companies can
build, sponsor, improve, and maintain.

## Vision

A world in which safe and useful AI is practical public-benefit infrastructure:
improving everyday life, strengthening human capability, and helping communities
develop positive habits and resilient services without exploitation, exclusion,
or loss of agency.

Differences between people and nations will always exist, but we live together
in one world. CloudSetup favours approaches that help people prosper together:
open standards, shared infrastructure, and cooperation across countries and
communities, over solutions that benefit one group at another's expense.
Knowledge and wisdom should be free, and access to AI and the internet should be
treated as a basic necessity for everyone.

## Mission

Identify high-friction problems affecting people and communities, investigate
existing solutions, and develop traceable opportunities and reusable projects
for safe, affordable AI-assisted tools.

`lived problem -> evidence -> responsible innovation -> accessible tool -> measurable benefit`

AI 2040 and other foresight work remain useful sources for understanding future
risks and possibilities. They are case studies, not the boundary of the mission.
The primary test is whether a project responsibly improves life now while
establishing positive patterns for the future.

## Goals

1. Discover important everyday problems whose existing solutions are expensive,
   inaccessible, fragmented, confusing, or absent.
2. Determine whether AI adds meaningful value; prefer a simpler non-AI tool when
   it can solve the problem more safely and economically.
3. Develop physical, digital, and commercial AI applications that augment people
   and service professionals rather than unnecessarily displacing them.
4. Make core tools free or affordable through open technology, sponsorship,
   grants, public programs, and sustainable commercial support.
5. Let organizations, entrepreneurs, researchers, and companies adopt or sponsor
   projects while protecting privacy, autonomy, safety, and community interests.
6. Replace harmful friction with opt-in assistance, informed choices, positive
   reinforcement, skill development, and stronger community capability.
7. Publish evidence, limitations, costs, tests, and stewardship clearly enough
   for others to reproduce, challenge, adopt, or improve the work.

## Opportunity areas

These examples express the direction; they are not automatic approvals:

- Scam, phishing, coercion, and fraud detection with understandable warnings and
  trusted verification paths at little or no cost.
- Mobility and accessibility tools for older adults and people with disabilities.
- Low-cost scanners and assistive sensing for interpreting objects, documents,
  environmental conditions, or equipment without covert surveillance.
- Plain-language navigators for benefits, bills, contracts, government forms,
  consumer rights, healthcare, and other high-friction services.
- Educational tools supporting students, parents, teachers, and institutions
  without replacing the human relationships central to learning.
- Tools reducing paperwork, invoicing, compliance, scheduling, and communication
  burdens for tradespeople, caregivers, and small businesses.
- Voluntary habit support for financial health, learning, wellness, and energy
  use, designed without manipulation or punitive scoring.
- Community tools for resilience, resource sharing, safety, waste reduction, and
  access to trusted information.

## Physical, commercial, and public-benefit AI

**Physical AI** includes AI-assisted devices and services that sense, move
through, or affect the physical world. Because failures can cause physical harm,
these projects require stronger testing, fail-safe behavior, accessibility
co-design, and regulatory review.

**Commercial AI** includes products and services that entrepreneurs or companies
can sustain economically. Commercial participation is welcome when it expands
access, quality, maintenance, and adoption. It must not depend on deceptive
design, sale of sensitive data, manufactured dependency, hidden discrimination,
or exclusionary pricing.

**Public-benefit AI** is the governing objective. Projects must create measurable
value, preserve meaningful human choice, and remain accountable when commercial
and public interests conflict.

## Foundry workflow

1. **Problem intake:** document the lived problem, affected community, present
   cost, and evidence without collecting unnecessary personal data.
2. **Landscape check:** identify existing tools, research, organizations,
   regulations, and open-source projects before suggesting new work.
3. **Assessment:** evaluate public value, accessibility, safety, AI necessity,
   feasibility, cost, adoption friction, and time-to-learning.
4. **Project package:** produce a bounded experiment, requirements, architecture,
   tests, cost model, risk controls, and stewardship plan.
5. **Build or contribute:** join existing work when possible; create a repository
   only for a demonstrated gap.
6. **Sponsorship:** support transparent milestones without purchasing conclusions
   or compromising user interests.
7. **Publication and adoption:** publish approved work with provenance,
   limitations, contribution paths, and a responsible maintainer.
8. **Learning loop:** measure benefit and harm, correct failures, and retire work
   that does not provide sufficient value.

## Public-interest safeguards

- Participation, personalization, and habit support are opt-in and reversible.
- AI assists people; it does not covertly manipulate behavior.
- Private data is minimized, protected, and processed locally when practical.
- Free services are not funded through hidden surveillance or sensitive-data
  sales.
- Medical, mobility, financial, legal, child-related, and public-safety tools
  require domain review, clear uncertainty, and human accountability.
- Educational and healthcare tools augment qualified people and institutions;
  they do not make unsupported diagnoses, prescriptions, or regulated decisions.
- Physical systems fail safely and offer a usable non-AI fallback.
- Affected people have a voice in design, evaluation, and correction.
- Projects disclose sponsors, conflicts, limitations, licenses, and ownership.

## Sponsorship compact

Sponsors may fund research, accessibility, compute, prototypes, pilots,
maintenance, or deployment. Sponsorship does not authorize suppression of
unfavorable results, capture of community data, weakened safeguards, or claims
that an experimental project is validated.

Each sponsored project defines its beneficiaries, sponsor interest, conflicts,
budget, milestones, acceptance tests, data and licensing terms, affordability
commitment, community feedback path, maintenance owner, and shutdown plan.

## Human–AI authorship

- AI proposes, analyzes, drafts, tests, and implements bounded work.
- Human owners, affected communities, and qualified reviewers set direction and
  approve consequential decisions.
- Published work distinguishes source claims, AI inference, human judgment, and
  verified implementation results.
- The system records concise evidence and decisions, not private chain-of-thought.

## Success

Success means less time or money lost to friction and fraud; greater mobility,
accessibility, understanding, independence, and safety; stronger learning and
self-directed habits; affordable access for underserved people; responsible
adoption; and reproducible evidence that benefits exceed foreseeable harm.

The objective is not the number of ideas generated. It is the number of safe,
useful, maintained improvements that reach people and make daily life better.
