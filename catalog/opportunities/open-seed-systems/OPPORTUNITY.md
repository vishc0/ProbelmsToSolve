# Opportunity: Open Seed Systems

> An open seed registry and evidence review makes variety, treatment, disease, and regulatory information inspectable.

## Owner direction

`[human-direction]` Preserve access to original or non-GMO seeds, investigate protective germination coatings and disease resistance, and curb harmful seed-system practices.

## Problem

Growers can struggle to compare seed provenance, variety traits, treatment evidence, disease risks, licensing terms, and locally applicable regulation across fragmented or proprietary sources.

## People

Growers, seed savers, breeders, researchers, extension services, regulators, cooperatives, and communities seeking resilient seed access.

## Root causes

- Concentrated intellectual property, distribution, and data control can limit alternatives (`cluster-concentrated-gatekeepers`, D).
- Seed production and disease resilience rely on specialized, interdependent systems with limited substitutes (`cluster-brittle-essential-systems`, J).

## Tool ideas from the plan

Build an open seed registry and evidence review covering open-pollinated and heirloom varieties, germination coatings, disease resistance, provenance, licensing, and regulation. Keep observed facts, study findings, and user reports distinct.

## Governance options

Compare public breeding, open-source seed licences, community seed banks, commercial breeding, benefit-sharing, and regulatory models on access, innovation, biosafety, farmer choice, and resilience.

## Guardrails

- Non-GMO preference is an owner direction, not an established safety conclusion; health, environmental, regulatory, and farmer-economic claims need separate evidence.
- Do not label a seed, coating, or practice safe or harmful without jurisdiction-specific evidence.
- Preserve provenance, licence terms, uncertainty, and expert review for agronomic guidance.
- Do not facilitate movement of restricted seed or biological material.
