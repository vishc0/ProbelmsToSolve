# Opportunity: Fisheries Livelihoods

> Transparent catch and income tools support fishers while evidence compares public, cooperative, and co-management models.

## Owner direction

`[human-direction]` Explore publicly run fisheries with fisher pay funded by government so livelihoods and long-term stewardship can be considered together.

## Problem

Fishers can face unstable income, concentrated buyers, fragmented catch data, ecological uncertainty, and rules that are difficult to reconcile with day-to-day livelihoods.

## People

Small-scale and commercial fishers, fishing communities, processors, cooperatives, scientists, Indigenous and customary rights holders, and public agencies.

## Root causes

- Concentrated access, purchasing, or licensing can leave fishers with few practical alternatives (`cluster-concentrated-gatekeepers`, D).
- Opaque allocation and pay systems can shift risk onto workers (`cluster-algorithmic-wage-suppression`, F).
- Livelihoods depend on fragile ecosystems, fleets, cold chains, and monitoring systems (`cluster-brittle-essential-systems`, J).

## Tool ideas from the plan

Develop a fisher-controlled income and catch-reporting tool, plus a reproducible evidence review of public, cooperative, rights-based, and co-management arrangements. Aggregate sensitive locations and route findings to people, not automated enforcement.

## Governance options

Compare public employment, cooperatives, co-management, quota and rights-based systems, income support, and market models on stock health, income stability, safety, local knowledge, rights, and administrative burden.

## Guardrails

- Public ownership and government-funded pay are owner directions, not established superior outcomes; they need country- and design-specific evidence.
- Protect precise fishing locations, commercially sensitive data, and community or Indigenous knowledge.
- Do not automate enforcement, quota sanctions, navigation, or safety-critical vessel decisions.
- Avoid stock, income, or policy-outcome guarantees.
- Keep trip and location data entirely local with no telemetry or remote synchronization.
- State that the tool is personal accounting support, not a substitute for Coast Guard manifests or statutory fisheries logbooks.

## Evidence

The P7 brief contrasts record fisheries and aquaculture output with overfished stocks, the dominance of small-scale fishers in employment, and harmful capacity-enhancing subsidies. It proposes private trip economics and lay-share auditing rather than automated enforcement.

### Prior art

- [FAO SSF Guidelines](https://www.fao.org/voluntary-guidelines-small-scale-fisheries/en/) establish human-rights and tenure protections for small-scale fishers.
- [Global Fishing Watch](https://globalfishingwatch.org/our-map/) provides open satellite AIS monitoring.
- Norway's [Garantikassen](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/1957-06-28-12) is a statutory minimum-earnings precedent for registered fishers during low-catch periods.

### Options and trade-offs

- Transferable quotas constrain catch and reward fleet efficiency, but can consolidate ownership and impose lease costs on working fishers.
- Community co-management preserves tenure and collective pricing power, but remains exposed to external poaching.
- State-funded fisher pay decouples income from catch volume, but entails fiscal commitment and catch auditing.
- A local logbook and pricing auditor supplies cost and wage evidence, but cannot change commodity prices by itself.
