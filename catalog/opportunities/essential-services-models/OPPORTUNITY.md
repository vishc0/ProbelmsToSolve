# Opportunity: Essential Services Models

> A neutral evidence explorer compares stewardship models for essential services on cost, access, quality, and outcomes.

## Owner direction

`[human-direction]` Examine government stewardship of industries where people's interests are at stake, including healthcare, pharmaceuticals, water, and fisheries, with taxation supporting pay and improvement cycles.

## Problem

Debates about essential services often compare labels rather than comparable outcomes, while people experience denial, high switching barriers, concentrated provision, and fragile capacity.

## People

Households, workers, patients, service operators, communities, policymakers, researchers, and institutions responsible for essential services.

## Root causes

- Denial processes can transfer service costs and burdens to individuals (`cluster-denial-by-default`, A).
- Concentrated providers can restrict meaningful alternatives (`cluster-concentrated-gatekeepers`, D).
- Capital-heavy essential systems can retain systemic single points of failure (`cluster-brittle-essential-systems`, J).

## Tool ideas from the plan

Build a neutral essential-services models explorer comparing public ownership, regulated utilities, cooperatives, nonprofits, and market models using reproducible indicators for cost, access, quality, resilience, workforce outcomes, and accountability.

## Governance options

Present public provision, public corporations, regulated private provision, cooperatives, mixed systems, procurement, and subsidy models under consistent definitions. Include financing, transition, oversight, capture, and political-independence risks.

## Guardrails

- Public ownership is a contested policy position and needs sector-, country-, and design-specific evidence rather than a presumed ranking.
- Keep observed outcomes separate from causal claims and owner preferences.
- Disclose dataset limits, funding models, time periods, and incomparable definitions.
- Do not provide individualized medical, legal, benefits, or investment advice.
- Anchor comparisons in audited financial and operational data, including shortcomings of both public and private models.
- Present CloudSetup as an educational and research organization, not a political advocacy group.

## Evidence

The P8 brief documents U.S. healthcare cost and overhead, international drug-price differences, water remunicipalization, medical debt, and public-production precedents. It motivates consistent empirical comparisons across stewardship models without treating ownership labels as outcomes.

### Prior art

- [CalRx](https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/OSPHLD/Pages/CalRx.aspx) is a state-contracted affordable biosimilar-insulin initiative.
- The [Public Futures database](https://publicfutures.org/cases) tracks de-privatisation and remunicipalisation across water, energy, and health.
- Municipal utility enabling laws, public-trust doctrines, and European public-benefit corporation models provide legal precedents.

### Options and trade-offs

- Unregulated investor ownership attracts venture investment and commercial incentives, but risks price extraction, denial of service, and administrative waste.
- Regulated investor-owned utilities use private capital, but face capture risk and rate-of-return incentives to overbuild.
- User or worker cooperatives provide democratic control and return surpluses to members, but can struggle to finance major infrastructure.
- Full public stewardship can prioritize universal access, workforce development, and resilience, but needs durable taxes and public performance metrics.
